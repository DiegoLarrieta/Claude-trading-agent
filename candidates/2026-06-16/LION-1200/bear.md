OBJECTIONS:

1. BROKEN ARITHMETIC — R:R IS 0.9:1, BELOW THE 1.2 EXPLORATORY FLOOR (HARD BLOCK)
   The technicals memo computes this explicitly: entry $15.85, stop $14.90, risk $0.95/share;
   target $16.70 (the 52-week high, the only charted level above), reward $0.85/share.
   R:R = 0.89:1. This is not a rounding edge case — it is NEGATIVE. The exploratory tier
   requires R:R ≥ 1.2; this misses by ~35%. The "lottery ticket on a takeout premium"
   argument is noted, but a takeout target has NO chart anchor — any price from $17 to $22
   could be argued, which means no price can be argued. A trade whose reward requires
   fabricating a target off the chart is not a trade with a 2:1 ratio; it is a trade with
   an imaginary ratio. The head trader would need to (a) cite a specific bid price with a
   source, and (b) show how a stop at $14.90 is consistent with holding through the
   volatility between here and that bid. Without that, the R:R is broken and this is a
   hard arithmetic objection regardless of tier.

2. CATALYST IS A SINGLE ANONYMOUS SOURCE WITH NO CORPORATE CONFIRMATION — RUMOR RISK IS
   MAXIMUM
   Load-bearing source re-fetched and confirmed: Semafor's article attributes the entire
   Lionsgate claim to "a person familiar with the matter" — one anonymous source. Netflix,
   Lionsgate, and Roku all declined to comment. No SEC filing, no formal indication of
   interest, no named banker, no named executive. The news.md evidence block correctly
   quotes this language, so no fabrication — but the memo's CONFIDENCE rating of "high" is
   not earned by a single anonymous source. High confidence describes the reliability of the
   outlet, not the reliability of the information. A single anonymous tip from even a
   credible outlet carries denial risk of close to 50%. If Netflix issues a "we are not in
   talks" statement — standard legal hygiene when a deal is NOT imminent — LION retraces
   5–8% overnight. That is not a tail risk; it is the base case for unconfirmed rumors.

3. THE 52-WEEK HIGH REJECTION IS AN EXHAUSTION SIGNAL, NOT A BREAKOUT
   Price printed $16.70 intraday — the exact 52-week ceiling — and closed $0.85 below it
   at $15.85. This is a tested-and-rejected high, the textbook exhaustion-spike pattern.
   The technicals memo correctly identifies it and flags the May 22 analog: LION ran +15.4%
   to $14.98 on 11.2M shares, then spent 18 trading days oscillating $13.21–$14.50 without
   reclaiming the high. Today's bar rhymes exactly. Buying into a rejected 52-week high the
   evening after the rejection — on a rumor — is buying the worst moment of the setup.
   Lesson #8 requires that the pop be confirmed on volume with a shelf held; the technicals
   memo explicitly says "no shelf confirmation" at the current close.

4. OVERNIGHT INTO FOMC IS A BINARY MACRO EVENT THAT SWAMPS THE NAME-SPECIFIC THESIS
   The FOMC decision prints tomorrow (June 17). Overnight gap risk is elevated for the
   entire market. A hawkish surprise or unexpected language change can gap LION through the
   $15.00 shelf before any stop can be filled — the real loss on a gap-through $14.90 could
   settle near $14.30 (today's intraday low) or lower, per the technicals memo. This
   transforms the already-broken R:R into something worse in expectation: the downside
   exceeds the charted stop, while the upside is capped by the same 52-week resistance.
   M&A rumors do not protect against macro gaps. This is not a judgment concern that the
   exploratory tier waives — it is a concrete mechanism by which the stop becomes
   unenforceable, which is an arithmetic problem, not a preference.

SEVERITY:
Objection #1 (broken arithmetic, R:R 0.9:1) is the single hardest block. The exploratory
tier's criteria per the 2026-06-12 authorization explicitly require that R:R not fall below
1.2 when computed honestly — and the technicals memo computes it honestly at 0.89:1. The
"lottery ticket" framing requires either (a) a specific, sourced takeout price that
produces the required ratio, or (b) accepting that the trade has no defined reward — which
is not a trade, it is a bet. Objection #4 (FOMC gap risk making the stop unenforceable)
compounds this: the denominator of the R:R ratio (the risk) is actually larger overnight
than $0.95 per share, making an already-broken ratio worse.

The M&A optionality is real: if Netflix submits a formal bid, the upside could be 30%+
and would not be capped at $16.70. I have considered this honestly. The problem is that
the exploratory tier does not authorize bets on binary unconfirmed rumors overnight into a
Fed meeting — it authorizes learning trades in structurally sound setups with honest R:R.
This setup has neither honest R:R nor a post-FOMC entry window. The firm can revisit LION
tomorrow after FOMC, if price opens and holds above $16.70 on confirming volume (actual
breakout, not probe), which would reset the geometry entirely.
