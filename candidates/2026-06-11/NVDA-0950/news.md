## READ-THROUGH: IS THIS ENTRY SANE?

**Verdict: YES, for a multi-year core hold; NO, for a trading position.**

**Why YES:**
- The demand case has **broadened and accelerated**, not narrowed. Oracle's capex raise is the most recent confirmation, but Nvidia's own $500B forward visibility and 75% sustained margins prove the orders are real and locked in.
- **Supply constraints remain real.** Nvidia's Blackwell ramp is not complete; Rubin is still ramping. Customers are fighting for allocation, not waiting for discounts. This is opposite of a mature, saturating market.
- **The valuation has compressed recently** (52-week low $140.85; entry $202.61 is 44% up from trough but still 14% below peak). A multi-year investor gets entry on relative weakness.
- **China export control overhang is now mostly priced in**. The $4.5B H20 charge is in the rear-view; guidance already factors in the $8B H20 revenue loss; Senate hearing today is a known event with limited surprise potential.
- **Competitive pressure from AMD is real but slow-moving.** No evidence of market share loss to AMD in near term. Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem moat remains durable.

**Why NO (if trading for 6–12 months):**
- **Sentiment is fragile.** Today's 1.71% move is on Oracle enthusiasm, but broader macro (inflation, Middle East tensions) has pressured tech all week. A 6% drop occurred earlier this week on Broadcom earnings and jobs data.
- **Concentration risk is the real tail risk.** If Meta or Google announce their custom chip deployment ahead of schedule (e.g., Q4 2026 vs. 2027), NVDA could gap down 20–30%.
- **Regulatory overhang is non-zero.** Senate hearing today could trigger a surprise crackdown on China loopholes, accelerating the capex cut the hyperscalers have telegraphed would follow.

**Primary evidence quality:** Multi-channel agreement (Bloomberg, Reuters, SEC filings, company guidance, industry analyst consensus) on demand thesis. Sources are predominantly primary (8-K filings, press releases, CFO commentary) rather than opinion. Oracle's $70B capex and Nvidia's own $500B forward visibility are THE strongest validation signals available.

**Conclusion for Diego's core-hold thesis:** This entry captures you at a moment of genuine demand acceleration (Oracle pivot) with reasonable valuation compression relative to forward visibility. The risks are known and priced at a 75% gross margin. For a 2–3 year hold through the Blackwell-to-Rubin transition, the asymmetry favors the long side. Do NOT expect smooth sailing; expect 15–25% pullbacks on macro events or competitive news (Burry's concentration call, AMD wins, China policy shocks). But the base case (sustained 40–50% Data Center growth, Rubin ramp 2026–2027, consumer TAM expansion) remains intact and has just gotten more credible with Oracle's capex raise.
