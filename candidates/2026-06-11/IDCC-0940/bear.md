OBJECTIONS:

1. **The sentiment memo's catalyst is wrong, and that's not a footnote — it's a process failure (Lesson #2 territory).** Sentiment claims "the stock rallied 14% on earnings that fell (EPS -35% YoY, revenue -2% YoY)" while news.md correctly identifies the catalyst as a same-day Amazon patent-licensing settlement, with no earnings event today (days_to_earnings = 49). I checked: IDCC's most recent reported quarter (Q1 2026, April 30) was a BEAT — GAAP EPS $2.14, non-GAAP $2.57 vs. ~$1.64 expected, revenue $205.4M vs. $194-200M guidance, ARR +13% YoY. The sentiment analyst's numbers don't match any recent IDCC quarter I can find — they appear to be either stale, hallucinated, or pulled from an unrelated dataset. Per Lesson #2 ("a dark/wrong data channel must be reported as such, never paraphrased to fill a gap"), this memo manufactured a false narrative ("crowd hasn't caught the thesis because they think earnings missed") on top of a fabricated premise. The "contrarian read" — that retail is asleep because it thinks this is a bad-earnings pop — is therefore worthless; we don't actually know why chatter is thin. This isn't a minor wording slip; it's a different catalyst entirely, and it went unchallenged into a memo that's supposed to gauge crowd psychology around the REAL news.

2. **The deal has zero disclosed economics — the market is pricing "strategic validation," not cash flow.** Both memos agree: terms are undisclosed, the matter moves to BINDING ARBITRATION to set final terms. A +14% move on 12.5x pace volume is a bet that arbitration will land favorably for IDCC. Arbitration outcomes are not announcements the market gets to see coming — and arbitrators frequently split the difference rather than awarding headline numbers. If the eventual arbitration award is modest (or takes 12-18 months, which is typical for patent arbitration), there's no fundamental anchor under today's price — the stock is trading on the *idea* of a deal, not the deal. The news analyst's "no red flags, appropriately sized" framing assumes the market has correctly priced an unknown number. That's not analysis, that's faith.

3. **Entry geometry is unfavorable and the analyst's own memo says so.** Technicals: ~5.3x ATR move already completed, price faded ~3.7% off the intraday high ($298.80 → $287.11) — a possible distribution signature within the climax. R:R to the nearest resistance (50-day MA, $300.82) is ~1:1 from current price, and technicals explicitly states "a pullback toward $275-280... would offer materially better geometry (R:R closer to 2:1+)." We are being asked to chase a move the analyst himself says we shouldn't chase at this price. Lesson #4: "Blind dip-buying [and by extension blind gap-chasing] backtests negative; selectivity is the entire edge." Entering now, with the analyst's own preferred level $7-12 below market, fails that test on its face.

4. **This "bounce" sits inside a six-month downtrend, and IDCC has a history of giving these gaps back.** Price is below both the 50-day ($300.82) and 200-day ($330.19) MAs, down from $410.77 (Oct 2025) to a $249.14 low two days ago — a ~39% drawdown from the highs. Technicals itself flags: "IDCC has shown repeated large single-day gaps (both directions) tied to licensing/litigation news throughout the year, so binary-event risk is real and today's gap could itself reverse intraday." A name with a track record of violent two-way litigation-driven gaps, currently in a structural downtrend, popping on a single piece of *unquantified* good news, is exactly the profile that round-trips. The 50-day MA overhead is real resistance from a real downtrend — not a level we're hoping holds, it's a level the stock has been rejected at before on the way down.

5. **Sparse, suspicious chatter cuts both ways — and "early movers were not retail" doesn't tell us WHO moved it.** Sentiment notes watcher count didn't spike and message volume is thin for a $7.4B name up 14% on huge volume. The memo spins this as "retail hasn't caught up yet" (bullish — more buyers coming). But thin retail chatter on a no-economics arbitration headline is equally consistent with: institutional desks already positioned ahead of the announcement (the deal didn't materialize from nothing — litigation has been ongoing since 2025) and are now using the pop to distribute into the headline. "Buy the rumor, sell the news" is a very old pattern for litigation settlements with undisclosed terms.

6. **Portfolio context: this would be a third consecutive day of overriding the committee's instinct to chase a fresh gap, on a name not even on Diego's declared watchlist (watchlist: false).** Of the two open positions, both (DNTH, CASY) were discretionary overrides of unanimous PASS verdicts, opened yesterday on "the move is overdone/justified" theses — both are still open and unproven, and SMCI (the third day-one trade, also a PASS-override "the selloff is overdone" bet) already stopped out at -5.9%. Per Lesson #6, the firm is explicitly tracking whether these overrides beat the committee, and so far the sample (1 closed, -5.9%) favors the committee. Adding a fourth gap-chase trade — this one with a fabricated sentiment narrative behind it — extends a pattern the firm has reason to be skeptical of, on a name that has no watchlist-thesis edge to fall back on.

SEVERITY: Objection #1 is the one that matters most. The sentiment memo is built on a factually wrong premise about what happened today (a real-earnings-miss narrative for a quarter that was actually a beat, on a day when the actual catalyst is unrelated litigation news). That means the "crowd is behind, more buyers coming" read — the only part of the bull case addressing who's left to buy — is unsupported. Strip that out and the trade is just "chase a 5+ ATR gap into 1:1 R:R resistance inside a downtrend on an arbitration headline with no disclosed terms," which the technicals analyst himself says is the wrong entry.
