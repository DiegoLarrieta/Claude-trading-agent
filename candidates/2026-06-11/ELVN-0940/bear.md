OBJECTIONS:

1. **This is a textbook chase-the-reversal entry — lesson #4, restated almost word for word by the firm's own technical analyst.** ELVN gapped +23% on news, immediately gave back over a third of that gap (high $45.52 → low $41.61, an 8.6% intraday reversal) on climactic 20x+ volume, and was in a documented 6-day downtrend (~$37.50 → $33.63) into 6/8 before today. The technicals memo explicitly states: "Neither version of this trade is a 'dip-to-support after established uptrend' setup; it is chasing a reversal off an extreme gap, which the desk's own track record (lesson 4) flags as low-edge." Lesson 4 says blind dip-buying/sharp-move-chasing backtests NEGATIVE and "interesting but unconvincing is a pass." The technical analyst handed the desk the pass on a silver platter and the only reason this memo exists is the news is good — but good news doesn't repeal the chart's statistics.

2. **The proposed tight stop is a near-certain noise stop-out, and the technicals memo says so directly.** The "defensible" $41.00 stop is only $0.88 below current price (~2.1%) — but that's just 0.4 ATR, against a stock that just moved 1.8 ATR in 40 minutes. The memo's own words: "a $41.00 stop is only 0.4 ATR below current price... so it is a high-probability stop-out on noise alone." The realistic alternative stop ($39.57, below the 5/29 swing low) drops R:R from 4.1:1 to 1.6:1 against the same target — and that target ($45.52, today's already-faded high) is itself unproven resistance from 90 minutes ago. Either the stop is too tight to survive the volatility this exact stock just demonstrated, or the trade's reward/risk collapses to barely-acceptable. There is no version of this entry geometry that is both safe and attractive.

3. **The 61% MMR / asciminib-ponatinib comparison is real but not as clean as the news memo frames it — and the memo undersells the de-risking that's already in the price.** I confirmed via primary search: ELVN-001's 80mg cohort showed 61% overall MMR (48% by 24 weeks) in a population where 67% had prior asciminib and 32% had prior ponatinib exposure — i.e., this is a heavily pretreated, multi-line population, and Enliven's own framing compares its *mature 80mg cohort* 38% MMR-by-24-weeks / 16% DMR figures to "precedent Phase 1 trials" of approved TKIs including asciminib — not to asciminib's pivotal ASCEMBL trial (25.5% MMR at week 24 in a similarly late-line, ≥2-prior-TKI population). The 61% headline figure mixes overall response with the 24-week comparable figure (48%), and the news memo's "61% vs asciminib's 24%" framing flattens this into a bigger gap than the company's own apples-to-apples comparison supports. This is still a good readout — but it is a Phase 1b, single-arm, open-label, n in the dozens, with FDA "alignment on trial design" (not approval, not even formal SPA) for a Phase 3 that hasn't started. A meaningful chunk of the de-risking thesis is "FDA likes the dose and population for a trial that begins H2 2026" — that is a long runway of Phase 3 execution risk, and the stock already re-rated +13-23% on the news. How much of the realistic incremental value is left to capture at $41.88 vs. the $33.63 base from three days ago?

4. **Sentiment is thin and the bullish "contrarian read" is itself doing the analyst's hoping for them.** Only 30 Stocktwits posts and 1,297 watchers on a name that just gapped 23% on a major efficacy readout is NOT "early discovery" — for a $2.6B biotech with an analyst PT raise (Stifel to $60) circulating, that's a *low* engagement level relative to the magnitude of the news, which could equally mean the broader market has already seen this, priced it during the open ($45.52 high), and is now selling into strength rather than "still early." The contrarian read picks the more bullish of two equally plausible interpretations of the same thin data — exactly the kind of unfalsifiable framing the firm should distrust.

5. **Process: the technicals memo's own conclusion ("genuinely unresolved... whether this stabilizes or continues fading toward $39-40") directly contradicts the news memo's confident "the move is not overreacted" framing.** One analyst says the chart doesn't know what it's doing yet; the other says the fundamentals justify exactly this price. Both can't be the basis for a high-conviction entry simultaneously — if the chart is genuinely unresolved, that argues for waiting for resolution, not entering into the uncertainty.

6. **Portfolio correlation note (minor):** this would be the third open position, and the second biotech (alongside DNTH, also a clinical-stage name carrying its own competitor-trial-failure overhang per lesson #5). A third small/mid-cap name concentrated in volatile, catalyst-driven biotech doesn't breach `max_total_exposure_usd` ($1400 cap; current exposure ~$340 + $200 = $540) or `max_trades_per_day` (this would be trade #1 today), so this is not a hard limit violation — but it does compound the firm's exposure to binary-event biotech risk on a day where the desk has already shown a pattern (lesson 6) of overriding committee passes on momentum names.

SEVERITY: Objection #1 is the one that matters most. The technical analyst — whose job is to find the entry, not argue against it — independently concluded this is a reversal-chase that the firm's own backtested experience (lesson 4) flags as negative-edge. When your own technicals memo says "this is exactly the pattern we said not to do," and the only counterargument is "but the news is really good," that is precisely the setup lesson 4 was written to prevent: good fundamental news does not change the statistics of buying a violently faded gap in a stock that was in a downtrend three days ago. Objection #2 (the stop) is the mechanical expression of #1 — there is no stop placement here that is both tight enough to be sized sanely and wide enough to survive the demonstrated volatility.
