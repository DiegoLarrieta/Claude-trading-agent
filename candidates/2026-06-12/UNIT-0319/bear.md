OBJECTIONS:

1. THERE IS NO CATALYST — this is a pure momentum/beta trade, and lesson #8 (momentum continuation has positive edge) does not apply here. News.md's own conclusion is "CATALYST: NONE FOUND company-specific." The +7.84% move is explained as (a) a Russell 2000 +~3% relief rally and (b) sector sympathy from AAOI/LITE/COHR. UNIT moved ~2.5-2.6x the index move — news.md flags this excess itself and cannot explain it ("some flow/positioning factor... that wire/trade press hasn't captured"). Buying into an unexplained 2.5x-beta excess move, on the THIRD consecutive day of a macro-relief rally that could mean-revert at any time, is buying the tail of someone else's trade, not a thesis about UNIT.

2. THE "SHELF HOLDING" STORY IS ONE DAY OLD AND THE SAMPLE SIZE IS ONE WICK. Technicals.md's bull case rests entirely on "Friday tested 12.45 (low 12.345) and reclaimed it within the first hour" — that is a single 37-minute observation as of the writing of the memo, on a thin, low-information morning. One recovered wick on the day after a gap is not "two tests, two holds" in any statistically meaningful sense — it's exactly what you'd expect from normal opening-range noise regardless of whether the level "means" anything. Technicals.md's own analog (3/31-4/01 breakout) is a SAMPLE SIZE OF ONE and is being used to imply this kind of breakout reliably holds in this name — that's a single data point dressed as a precedent.

3. THE R:R IS BAD ON THE TRADE AS ACTUALLY FRAMED. Technicals.md admits the immediate target (52w high 12.645) gives R:R ≈ 0.4-0.5:1 — a losing proposition on its own. The memo then reframes the target to the round number 13.00 (not a chart level, explicitly "a round-number reference... there is no overhead supply data to lean on") to manufacture a 2.25:1 R:R. We are being asked to size a stop against a real level (12.45 shelf) but size a target against a number that exists only because it ends in two zeros. If the stop is real and the target is imaginary, the true R:R is closer to 0.4-0.5:1.

4. SENTIMENT IS THIN AND THE "BULLISH" READ IS BUILT ON THE WRONG DATA — and more importantly, sentiment.md is FIVE+ HOURS STALE relative to news.md/technicals.md. It was written pre-open (~3am ET) and reflects a "30 posts in 71 days" Stocktwits sample with the bullish tone driven by two earnings-recap accounts and a single small account (4,039 followers) calling "12 land." This tells us almost nothing about today's live order flow, the actual buyer/seller balance during the live shelf-test technicals.md describes, or whether retail is now piling in behind the breakout (the "who's left to buy" question is unanswered, not resolved bullish).

5. WATCH-LEVEL TRIPWIRE = DECLARED BIAS, AND THE THESIS DOESN'T SURVIVE A STRANGER TEST. Diego set a tripwire at 12.45 — UNIT is pre-selected as a name he's predisposed to like, exactly the situation that demands harder scrutiny per the declared-bias protocol. Strip away "Diego already had his eye on this" and what's left is: a small-cap REIT-like fiber name made a 52-week high on no news, riding a macro rally, with one good wick-recovery and a thin sentiment sample. If a stranger ticker walked in with this exact memo set, would the desk take it? The case reads like "I was already watching this and it broke my level" — that is exactly the framing the bias-check exists to catch.

Lesson #8 carved out an exception for MEASURED momentum continuation (a backtested mechanical rule: +4% pop on 2.5x volume, fixed stop/target/hold), not for discretionary entries into 52-week-high breakouts with no fundamental story and a manufactured target.

7. PORTFOLIO CONCENTRATION / CORRELATION CHECK. The portfolio already holds CASY (a "buy the pop" override, currently +$24.70 unrealized) and F (a plumbing drill). UNIT would be a THIRD position opened almost entirely on "the tape is strong and a level held" logic — if the macro relief rally that's lifting UNIT, CASY, and broad small-caps reverses (Iran de-escalation headlines are notoriously reversible), all of these long, beta-sensitive, momentum-justified positions take the same hit at the same time. The memos don't address this correlation; technicals.md frames UNIT's move as "high-beta micro-cap riding the macro relief rally" — that beta exposure is not unique to UNIT, it's a portfolio-level bet on the rally continuing, stacked across multiple names.

SEVERITY: Objection #1 is the one that matters most. Every other objection (the thin sentiment, the manufactured 13.00 target, the one-wick "shelf hold," the bias check) is downstream of the same root problem — there is no company-specific reason UNIT should be up 7.84% on 2.57x volume, and the analysts confirm this rather than dispute it. Without a catalyst, "the chart looks good and a level held" is exactly the discretionary chase pattern the firm has already lost money on once this week (SMCI) and flagged twice in lessons.md (#4, #6). A 52-week-high breakout with no news, on day 3 of a fragile geopolitical relief rally, is the textbook setup for a fast reversal if the macro tailwind stalls — and nothing in these memos would tell us in advance if that's about to happen.
