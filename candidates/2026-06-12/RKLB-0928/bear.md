OBJECTIONS:

1. "Wait for the shelf" is not a trade proposal — it's a different trade with no entry. Technicals' own numbers say the CURRENT setup is a 1.2:1 R:R (stop ~$99.50, ~6.6% risk, vs. ~7.8% reward to $114.78). Per lesson #4 ("down X% on a sector headline, still above support is not a buy signal") and lesson #6 (selectivity is the edge, "interesting but unconvincing" is a pass), a 1.2:1 R:R buy-the-gap-down trade is exactly the pattern that backtested negative. If the thesis is "this becomes attractive once it reaches ~$101 with a reversal candle," then the correct action TODAY is no trade — set a price alert, not a limit order at current price. Any proposal to buy now and call the shelf "the stop" is dressing up a marginal-R:R momentum-fade as a support trade it hasn't earned yet.

2. The load-bearing Yahoo Finance article, re-fetched, confirms the quotes in news.md are accurate — but it ALSO undersells the most relevant fact buried in news.md's own second source. The Stocktwits/Farrar piece (also cited, also re-fetched) states explicitly: "Rocket Lab operates its own Electron rocket, but its larger Neutron vehicle will compete against Falcon 9 in a market where SpaceX already enjoys substantial scale advantages" and Farrar says he's "not convinced there's a viable scenario where SpaceX dominates launch but still has lots of effective competition downstream." News.md's READ-THROUGH frames this as "not new information" because it was debated during the May 29 episode — but RKLB's Neutron (the vehicle that would actually compete with Falcon 9) is delayed to Q4 2026 after a January tank-rupture/manufacturing failure (not mentioned in any memo). SpaceX going public as a $1.77T comp with disclosed scale economics is the first time the market gets to put a number on that competitive gap in a liquid, tradeable way. This is at minimum a plausible START of a re-rating, not provably "just flow" — and the memo's confidence that it's "just flow" rests on an analogy to a smaller prior episode (May 29: ASTS -11%, RDW -4%, RKLB/LUNR -2%) where RKLB's relative damage was much smaller than today's -7.7%. The pattern argues the OPPOSITE of "overreaction" — RKLB is taking proportionally MORE damage this time than last time on the same narrative.

3. Today's move is a near-complete round trip of yesterday's pop, not a fresh, isolated shock. Per my web search: RKLB rose ~9% on June 11 (close $114.78) on SpaceX IPO-pricing excitement, and is now giving almost all of that back (-7.7% to ~$106) on the actual pricing/listing. "Buy this dip" is actually "buy back the same stock the crowd bought yesterday at a worse average price, on the same catalyst, after the catalyst resolved." That is not sector-sympathy panic on unrelated bad news — it's the SAME catalyst round-tripping, which is a much weaker "overreaction" story than news.md presents (news.md frames it purely as "yesterday's buyers underwater," but doesn't connect that RKLB itself was one of yesterday's biggest beneficiaries of the same hype it's now reversing).

4. Sentiment is internally stale/contradicted by outside data the memo didn't have: sentiment.md reports 75% bullish-tagged posts (9 of 12 tagged, n=30, 9-minute window) and calls this "constructive." My search turned up independent reporting that "Stocktwits retail sentiment for RKLB has plunged to 'bearish' from 'bullish' a week ago" — a broader, longer-window read that contradicts the snapshot. The memo's own sample is tiny (12 tagged posts) and the top-follower author (60k followers) is flagging institutional selling (IBD heavily-sold list), not bullish. If the crowd has actually flipped net bearish over the past week, "the crowd is behind the move, not ahead of it" cuts the other way — there may be more sellers left than the memo implies, not fewer.

5. Nasdaq-100 inclusion as a "positive idiosyncratic catalyst that was overridden" cuts both ways. Index inclusion triggers forced buying from index funds, but that's a known, dated, mechanical event (effective June 22) — it does not require buying TODAY, and sophisticated holders may be selling INTO the inclusion run-up that already happened (RKLB ran from ~$78 to $151 over five weeks per technicals.md) rather than buying ahead of it. Treating "good news got overridden by a bad sector day" as proof the sector move is irrational ignores that some of today's selling could be profit-taking BY THE PEOPLE WHO ALREADY GOT THE INCLUSION POP, using the SpaceX IPO as cover/liquidity.

6. Process contradiction between news.md and technicals.md: news.md's READ-THROUGH concludes the -7.7% move is "plausibly an overreaction... given RKLB sits at the lower end of the sector's decline range (-6% to -15%) despite being the largest-cap, most 'blue chip' name" — implying RKLB held up BETTER than peers, which it spins as bullish (less damage = less panic = more overreaction-prone = better bounce). But technicals.md's NOTES section explicitly flags this as unverified ("check whether peers are down a similar magnitude; if RKLB is down meaningfully MORE than peers, that's a negative tell") and does not confirm it. News.md is asserting as settled the exact comparison technicals.md says still needs checking — two memos disagreeing on a load-bearing fact (relative sector performance) that the trade's "overreaction" framing depends on.

7. RKLB closed candidate flagged watchlist: false — this name is NOT on Diego's predisposed-thesis list, so the normal "scrutinize the bias harder" caveat doesn't directly apply, but it also means there's no standing edge/familiarity argument in its favor; this has to stand purely on today's setup, and per objection #1, today's setup (1.2:1 R:R, price above support) is the exact shape that historically loses.

8. Volatility regime risk: technicals.md notes RKLB "round-tripped from ~$78 to $151 and back to ~$106.5 in roughly five weeks" — ATR14 of $11.86 on a ~$106 stock is >11% daily range. A "tight" stop at $99.50 (6.6% risk) is less than one ATR; in a stock this volatile, a stop that close has a meaningfully elevated chance of being tagged by noise before the thesis (sector calms down, shelf holds) has time to play out — independent of direction.

SEVERITY: Objection #1 is the one that matters most. Every other objection is a reason to doubt the bull case's quality; objection #1 is a reason the TRADE AS STRUCTURED ISN'T A TRADE AT ALL. Technicals.md itself says the current R:R (1.2:1) is marginal and that the GOOD trade (>3:1) only exists if/when price reaches ~$101 and shows a reversal. If the head trader proposes buying now at ~$106 with a stop near $99.50, they are buying a 1.2:1 setup that the firm's own backtest data (lesson #6) says is a loser, dressed in the language of a setup ($101 shelf + reversal) that hasn't happened yet. The other objections (#2, #3, #4, #6) all chip away at the "this is just irrational sympathy panic" framing that's the only thing that would make a sub-2:1 entry justifiable on conviction alone.
