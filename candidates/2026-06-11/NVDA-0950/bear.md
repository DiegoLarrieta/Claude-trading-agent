OBJECTIONS:

1. **The news memo's central "event risk is known and bounded" framing rests on a fact that is wrong: Jensen Huang is NOT testifying today.** The memo states "CEO Jensen Huang summoned to testify before Senate Banking Committee on June 11, 2026 on export control compliance" and treats this as "a known event with limited surprise potential." In fact, Huang **declined** Sen. Warren's invitation (reported June 8), and the actual witnesses at today's 10am Dirksen hearing ("AI and the American Dream") are think-tank analysts (ITI, Hudson Institute, AEI) — not Nvidia leadership. This isn't a minor detail: a CEO testifying under oath about export-control compliance is a materially different risk than a policy hearing NVDA isn't even a party to. The memo's reassurance that "Senate hearing today is a known event with limited surprise potential" is built on a catalyst that doesn't exist as described. What the news analyst doesn't know: Warren is actively escalating (she's also pushed Commerce to suspend/reconsider NVDA export licenses amid the Supermicro chip-diversion indictment, and criticized a "closed-door meeting" Huang held instead). A CEO who ducks public testimony while regulatory pressure visibly escalates is, if anything, a higher-tail-risk signal than a calendar event that resolves cleanly today — it means the overhang does NOT resolve today and keeps building. Today buys you exposure to an open-ended Washington narrative the news memo materially mischaracterized as "priced in and resolving now."

2. **"Watch the bull case as if NVDA were a stranger" — the technicals memo's own conclusion is that today is not the entry, and the only reason to override that is the watchlist bias.** The technicals memo is unusually direct: "today is a 'reasonable but not optimal' entry," "today's entry is NOT at support," waiting for $194.5-$197 (May base) or the 200-day MA "would meaningfully improve entry geometry... without giving up much of the long-term thesis." Strip away the watchlist framing and ask: would the firm initiate a brand-new $200 core position in a stranger ticker, mid-pullback, 1.4% below the 50-day MA (immediate overhead resistance), on a memo that explicitly says a better entry is 4-7% away and "wouldn't give up much"? No — that's the textbook definition of "wait." The fact that this is Diego's name doesn't change the chart. Lesson #4 (blind dip-buying backtests negative; selectivity is the edge) applies directly: this is "buy because I want in," not "buy because the setup is here."

3. **The proposed stop ($192, -5.5%) sits ABOVE the support level the technicals memo identifies as the real line ($194.5 May swing-low / $188.91 200-day MA confluence) — wait, actually it's BELOW $194.5 but ABOVE the 200-day MA, placing it inside the support zone rather than below it.** The memo frames $192 as "below the May 4-5 swing-low cluster ($194.5) and the rising 200-day MA ($188.91)" but $192 > $188.91 — the stop is actually ABOVE the 200-day MA, not below it. A normal test-and-hold of the 200-day MA (which the memo itself calls a "confluence zone" and a totally normal pullback target for an uptrend 7.5% above that MA) could tag $192 and stop the position out on noise, before the thesis is even tested. For a "months-long core hold" sized to survive 15-25% pullbacks (per the news memo's own expectation), a 5.5% stop that sits inside the support confluence zone is geometrically the worst of both worlds: too tight to survive the volatility the bull case itself predicts, but marketed as "wide."

4. **Customer concentration (39% from 2 customers, up from 25% a year ago) is trending the WRONG direction, and the news memo's mitigant ("hard to walk away mid-contract") is asserted, not sourced.** A rising concentration ratio into a smaller number of hyperscalers who are ALL simultaneously building custom silicon (Meta, Google, Amazon, Microsoft — the memo names all four as in-progress) is the single most binary risk in the bull case: a 20-30% gap on a custom-chip acceleration announcement is explicitly flagged by the news memo itself as plausible. For a core position meant to be held through exactly this kind of headline, "the $500B forward visibility suggests commitments are locked in" is the kind of comfort that evaporates the day one hyperscaler trims guidance — and nothing in any memo checks whether any of the four have signaled a near-term custom-chip deployment timeline pull-forward (which is precisely the kind of search a bear should do and the news memo did not).

5. **Uniform three-way agreement, on a watchlist name, with the technicals memo simultaneously saying "not optimal," is itself a flag.** Lesson #6 says the firm should track whether discretionary overrides outperform committee process. Here the override isn't even needed for direction — all three memos like NVDA — the override is needed to buy TODAY rather than wait for the better-documented entry the firm's own technical analyst identifies four points lower. That's a different kind of override than DNTH/CASY (where the committee disagreed on direction). Here the committee effectively agrees with Diego on "NVDA, yes" and only diverges on "today, at $202.61, vs. $194-197." If the thesis is genuinely a multi-year core hold, the cost of waiting for the technicals memo's own preferred entry is small (a few percent of upside foregone, against meaningfully better risk geometry) — so why not wait? The honest answer the technicals memo all but states is: there isn't a trading reason to buy today over $194-197 — only an impatience reason.

6. **Sentiment is "bullish but not euphoric" — read correctly, this means there's no capitulation, no washout, and no contrarian edge to today specifically.** The sentiment memo's own contrarian read says retail "hasn't priced in the full thesis" and frames this as favorable for patience — but it cuts the other way too: if the crowd isn't yet excited about today's bounce specifically (76% bullish is roughly NVDA's baseline good-name sentiment, not a spike), then today's +1.71% isn't a sentiment-driven dislocation worth buying into — it's just Tuesday. There is no crowd mispricing being exploited by buying at $202.61 specifically vs. $195.

7. **Portfolio context: this would be the 4th discretionary override in 2 trading days, and 2 of the prior 3 (DNTH, CASY) were already "buy at a worse price than the analysts' preferred level" trades that happened to work.** A pattern of "the committee says wait/pass, Diego buys now anyway, and it's worked twice" is exactly the kind of small-sample variance Lesson #6 warns is being tracked — and a third instance in the same vein (buy now vs. the technicals memo's documented better entry) compounds the position-sizing and cash-management story: $200 into NVDA today plus the existing DNTH/CASY exposure (both still open, both unrealized) pushes total exposure higher while $194-197 remains realistically reachable within days given NVDA hasn't even finished its current pullback per the technicals memo's own "dip-to-support (in progress, not yet at support)" language.

SEVERITY: Objection #1 is the one that matters most. The entire "this is a sane core entry today, not in a week" argument leans on the Senate hearing being a clean, bounded, resolving-today event ("limited surprise potential"). That premise is factually wrong — Huang isn't appearing, the political pressure is escalating rather than resolving, and the hearing happening today does NOT retire this overhang the way the news memo implies. Combined with objection #2 (the technicals memo's own preferred entry is $194-197, days away in an "in progress" pullback), the case for buying TODAY specifically — as opposed to waiting — is weaker than any memo states outright.
