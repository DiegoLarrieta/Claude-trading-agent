OBJECTIONS:
1. The breakdown is not "fully priced in" — it's a two-day, two-stage repricing (-7.6% Jun 9, -17.85% Jun 10) and the news memo's own sources show the selloff *accelerated* on the second day. A $7B raise (7%+ dilution plus a mandatory convertible preferred that converts in 3 years) is a structural overhang, not a one-day digestion event. There is no evidence the market has finished re-rating SMCI for permanently higher share count and future preferred-conversion supply. Calling -17.85% on top of -7.6% an "overreaction" with only one day of reaction time is a guess dressed as a conclusion.

2. The "support" at $32.00/MA50 is hopeful, not demonstrated. Technicals admit price is "13.4% below MA20" with the "20/50 not yet inverted but MA20 starting to slope down" — i.e., a death cross is forming. The memo's own playbook says if $32 breaks, next stop is $30–$30.85, then $29.47, then the $19.48 52-week low. We are proposing to buy *into* a confirmed breakdown from a textbook distribution top ($51.40 → $33.39, -35% from high in under two weeks) with the stop just $1.51 below entry — that's a coin-flip stop placement on a name in active technical deterioration, not a defined edge.

3. The sentiment analyst's "contrarian read" (institutional panic/margin-call capitulation, oversold bounce likely) is explicitly labeled as guesswork: "Reddit API unavailable; assessed via price action and volume volatility pattern" — this is not crowd data, it's the technicals memo restated as if it were independent confirmation. Two of three memos are effectively the same observation (price/volume pattern) counted twice to manufacture conviction.

4. SMCI-specific tail risk the analysts didn't address: this is a company with a recent history of accounting scandal, auditor resignation (EY in 2024), delayed 10-K filings, and a near-Nasdaq-delisting episode within the last two years. A surprise $7B emergency-style capital raise the day after still being near 52-week highs is exactly the pattern that historically preceded SMCI's prior credibility crises. None of the three memos checked for fresh accounting/audit/governance headlines accompanying this raise — that's the negative source most likely to be missing.

5. Volume context is weaker than framed: "1.29x average volume" and "0.80x ATR" for a -17.85% single-day move is unusual — a move of this magnitude on sub-ATR range and only modestly elevated volume could mean the order book is thin and air-pocketed (gap risk), not "calm capitulation." Thin liquidity into a falling knife makes a $31.50 stop more likely to gap through on the next leg down rather than execute cleanly at a $2.01 loss.

6. Position sizing math: $200 trade size on a $33.385 entry buys ~5.99 shares; a $31.50 stop caps loss at ~$11.30 (5.66% of the $200 allocation, 1.13% of total $1,000 capital) — survivable in isolation, but this is trade #1 of the day into a name actively breaking down with a structural dilution overhang and a possible governance landmine. Spending 1 of 3 daily trade slots and 20% of total deployable capital on the most binary-risk name in today's 31-candidate batch is a poor risk/reward allocation when calmer setups likely exist in the same batch.

SEVERITY: Objection #4 (undisclosed SMCI-specific governance/accounting tail risk) is the one that matters most — it's the single factor that could turn a "buy the dip on a strong backlog" thesis into a "this is the start of a much larger unwind" thesis, and it's the one piece of evidence none of the three analysts went looking for, despite SMCI's well-documented history of exactly this kind of event preceding much larger drawdowns.
